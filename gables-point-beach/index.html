Southern shoreline

Gables Point beach, Point Vernon

Last checked: 14/08/2026

Gables Point is the longest of the four Beachsafe-listed beach sections associated with Point Vernon, running along the suburb’s south-facing foreshore.

Around the southern side

Which part of the coast is Gables Point beach?

Beachsafe uses “Gables Point” for an approximately 3.5-kilometre south-facing beach backed by the Esplanade and a continuous foreshore reserve. It is separate from the shorter east-facing Point Vernon beach and from Gatakers Bay.

The beach record is much longer than The Gables park. Do not assume every reference to Gables Point means the picnic area beside the historic site.

A tide-shaped shoreline

What is it like?

Beachsafe describes a steep, narrow high-tide beach fronted by linear rock flats approximately 50 to 100 metres wide. The usable strip of sand and the distance to the water therefore change substantially through the tide.

Different access points can feel like different beaches even though Beachsafe groups them in one long record. Park names, street names and local descriptions remain useful for saying exactly where you mean.

Access, parking and facilities

Beachsafe lists formal parking, drinking water, toilets and a boat ramp within the full 3.5-kilometre beach record. That does not mean every facility is present at The Gables park or every street access.

The Gables park does not list a toilet

Council’s current park record lists a barbecue, picnic shelters, seating, a water tap and dog-bag dispenser. It does not list a toilet. Check the exact park or access point before relying on the broader Beachsafe facility list.

Eastern-end boating access

The Gables/Aplin Street ramp

Council lists the Point Vernon ramp at Aplin Street on the eastern side of the suburb and marks it as low-tide restricted. The ramp is not a general swimming access: keep clear of launching and retrieving vessels.

A predicted tide height alone cannot confirm safe access for a particular boat. Boaters still need current weather, ramp conditions, navigation information and knowledge of their vessel.

Why the name matters

The Gables was also a boarding house

The place name preserves the memory of a large wooden boarding house built in 1888 as Bayview Boarding House, later called Vernon and then The Gables. The building was demolished around 1957, but the name remains attached to the park and coastal area.

Nearby shoreline remains associated with Dunn’s Enclosure, often remembered as the Point Vernon rock baths. That history is distinct from the beach’s current safety and access information.

Unpatrolled shoreline

Tide and water-safety essentials

  • Check tide, weather and warnings before entering the water or walking onto flats.
  • Allow for the return route to change as the tide rises.
  • Wear secure footwear on wet rock and shell.
  • Keep children within reach around water and slippery edges.
  • Keep out of the boat-ramp approach.
  • Use current signs for dog access; the whole 3.5-kilometre beach is not governed by one simple off-leash rule.
