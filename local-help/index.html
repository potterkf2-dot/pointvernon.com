Police and immediate danger

Is it an emergency?

Call Triple Zero (000)

Use 000 when someone is seriously injured, a life or property is being threatened, or a crime is happening now and police, fire or ambulance attendance is urgent.

Non-urgent police

Use Policelink online or call 131 444 when police do not need to attend immediately.

Hooning or traffic complaint

Use the QPS reporting form or call 134 666. If danger is immediate, call 000.

Lost property

Use Queensland Police’s lost-property option through Policelink rather than posting identifying details publicly.

Suspicious activity

Use the relevant Policelink pathway. Do not confront someone or publish an accusation in a community group.

Property crime and evidence

Break-in, theft or useful CCTV

If a crime is happening now, the suspect is still at the scene or anyone is in immediate danger, call 000. After the event, use the matching Queensland Police online form or call Policelink on 131 444.

Use the specific report

Policelink has separate pathways for break-ins, vehicle break-ins, stolen property and stolen vehicles. Keep the reference number for updates and insurance.

Send evidence securely

For an online offence report with available photographs or video, QPS says it sends an email or SMS with a secure evidence-upload link. Use that link rather than publishing CCTV or accusations on Facebook.

Reduce easy opportunities

QPS recommends locking doors, windows, garages, sheds and vehicles; keeping keys and valuables out of sight; and recording or photographing identifiable property.

Choose a QPS reporting form or review the QPS home-security guide.

Different problem, different pathway

Hooning, e-bikes and local-road concerns

  • Dangerous behaviour happening now: call 000 when driver or rider behaviour is actively life-threatening.
  • Speeding, burnouts or reckless driving: Fraser Coast Regional Council says enforcement belongs to Queensland Police. Use the QPS hooning form or call 13 HOON (134 666). If it is safe, note the vehicle, registration, time, date and place.
  • Road design or traffic calming: Council can assess non-urgent local-road concerns, but says traffic-calming devices are not suitable everywhere and are not a substitute for enforcement. Lodge the location and concern through the Council request portal.
  • E-bikes and e-scooters: Queensland's rules from 1 July 2026 set a 12 km/h maximum on footpaths and require riders to slow to 12 km/h when passing pedestrians on shared paths. Legal e-bike motors are limited to 250 watts continuous output and assistance up to 25 km/h. Check the official page for current rules, including further age and licensing changes scheduled from 31 August 2026.

Check Queensland's current e-bike, e-scooter and wheeled-device rules.

Local infrastructure and public places

Report a Council issue

Fraser Coast Regional Council’s request portal accepts reports about trees, paths, graffiti, dumped rubbish, bins, water faults, park maintenance, barking dogs and other Council services.

The portal sends the issue and location to the relevant team and provides a reference number. You can also call Council on 1300 79 49 29.

Open Council’s request-a-service page

Household waste and fire prevention

Dispose of batteries safely

Fraser Coast Regional Council says batteries and battery-powered items must never go in household waste or recycling bins. Use an appropriate battery-recycling or waste-facility collection point.

  • Check more than loose batteries: vapes, electric toothbrushes, toys, Bluetooth speakers, smart watches and power tools can contain embedded batteries.
  • Find a collection point: use Council’s embedded-battery guidance or the B-cycle drop-off finder for current options and acceptance conditions.
  • Do not rely on the bin type: Council’s warning applies to both household waste and recycling bins because damaged or compacted batteries can ignite.

Read Council’s embedded-battery guidance or find a B-cycle drop-off point.

Parks, beaches and overnight stays

Foreshore camping, fires or dumped rubbish

Fraser Coast Regional Council says camping is permitted only in designated campgrounds or tourist parks. Overnight stays in Council-controlled parks, road reserves or car parks—including sleeping in a vehicle—may be illegal.

  • Suspected illegal camping: use Council’s official illegal-camping report or call 1300 79 49 29. Do not confront occupants.
  • Fire in a Council park or foreshore: Council’s local law permits a fire only where it is approved, in a designated fireplace or authorised in writing. Call 000 if a fire is out of control or threatens people or property; otherwise report it to Council.
  • Rubbish, damage or blocked access: send the location and factual details through Council’s request portal.

Check lawful camping options or report illegal camping.

Living in a caravan on private property

Private land does not make every caravan stay unrestricted. Council’s no-approval pathway is for an owner or occupier allowing family or friends to stay in a caravan or recreational vehicle for up to six months in a twelve-month period. Council must be notified within seven days, and conditions apply to the permanent residence, setbacks, sanitation, services, mobility and occupancy.

A paid arrangement, a stay by someone outside that family-or-friends pathway, or use while building a dwelling may involve different Council approval and tenancy requirements. Check the exact proposal with Council before agreeing to the stay or paying rent.

Read Council’s temporary-home and caravan requirements.

Air quality and open fires

Smoke, backyard fire or odour

Fraser Coast Regional Council says backyard fires must comply with current fire bans and restrictions and must not create a smoke nuisance or fire hazard.

  • Before lighting a fire: check the Queensland Fire Department’s current fire-ban information. Council’s fact sheet says a fire greater than two metres in any direction requires a Permit to Light Fire.
  • What may be burned: Council says commercial, domestic, green and recyclable waste must not be burned in non-rural areas. Permitted fire pits and cooking appliances must use a suitable fuel such as clean, dry firewood and must not cause a smoke impact.
  • Smoke or odour nuisance: Council can investigate some residential smoke and odour complaints. Call Environmental Health through Council on 1300 79 49 29.
  • Fire threatening people or property: call 000.

Read Council’s smoke and odour guidance or check Queensland fire bans and restrictions.

Domestic animals and wildlife

Lost pet, wandering animal or injured wildlife

Lost pet

Check Council’s live impounded-animal list and contact the Animal Facility on 1300 79 49 29. Proof of ownership and photo identification are required to reclaim an animal.

Found dog or cat

Council accepts found domestic animals at the Fraser Coast Adoption Centre and Pound in Cicada Lane, Urangan, during its published opening hours. A vet can check for a microchip.

Ranger pickup

Council lists weekday pickup hours and requires someone to be present. Call 1300 79 49 29 for the current arrangement.

Injured or neglected animal

Call RSPCA Queensland on 1300 264 625 (1300 ANIMAL). Avoid handling wildlife unless the service advises you to do so.

Snake in or around a home

Keep people and pets well clear. Do not try to catch, handle or kill the snake. If it is indoors, close internal doors and open an external exit if this can be done safely, then contact a licensed snake catcher. Treat any suspected bite as an emergency: call 000 and keep the person still while waiting for an ambulance.

Possible crocodile sighting

Keep clear and report a direct sighting through the QWildlife app, the Queensland Government’s online form or by calling 1300 130 372 (option 1). The department assesses public reports; a Facebook post is not official confirmation of a local sighting. Obey any on-site warning sign.

Persistent barking dog

Fraser Coast Regional Council encourages contacting the dog owner first only if it is safe to do so. If that is not possible or the barking continues, lodge a service request through Council rather than posting the address or an accusation publicly.

  • Council first reviews the report and may ask you to complete a noise questionnaire.
  • If a formal investigation begins, a Community Ranger may use listening surveys, neighbourhood enquiries, noise-monitoring equipment or a noise diary.
  • Give Council factual dates, times, durations and the effect of the noise. Additional evidence may be required before the matter can progress.

Lodge a Council service request, read Council’s barking-dog guidance or open Council’s Barking Dog Information Pack (PDF).

Read Council’s current lost-and-found process

Read Queensland’s guidance about living with snakes or check official snake-bite first aid

Electrical safety and outages

Power outage or fallen line

  • Life-threatening electrical emergency: call 000 or Ergon Energy on 13 16 70.
  • Lost power or supply problem: check Ergon’s Outage Finder or call 13 22 96.
  • Outage notifications: register for Ergon Energy Customer Self Service, add the NMI from the electricity bill and choose SMS or email alerts. Ergon says planned-outage alerts are normally sent five days before and again on the morning; unplanned-outage alerts cover loss and restoration of supply. Check Outage Finder if an alert is delayed.
  • Stay well clear of fallen powerlines and anything touching them. Treat every line as live.

Open Ergon Energy’s outage and safety page or manage SMS and email notifications.

Flood, storm and severe weather

Current warnings and storm help

Use the Fraser Coast Disaster Dashboard for current local emergency information and follow instructions from emergency services.

  • Life-threatening emergency: call 000.
  • Non-life-threatening flood or storm assistance: request Queensland SES help online, through the SES Assistance QLD app or by calling 132 500.
  • Never drive, ride, walk or swim through floodwater. Stay clear of fallen powerlines and damaged structures.

Open the Fraser Coast Disaster Dashboard

On the water

Marine emergency, assistance and trip logging

In a marine emergency, use VHF channel 16 or call 000. Marine Rescue Queensland lists 131 MRQ (131 677) as its phone contact and VHF channel 73 for Hervey Bay log on and log off.

MRQ Hervey Bay publishes current radio hours, channels and unit details on its official page. Check them before departure rather than relying on an old saved number.

Check MRQ Hervey Bay

Useful evidence, safer reporting

Report facts without creating a public incident file

  • Record the time, place and what you directly observed.
  • Give identifying details, photographs or video to the responsible authority through its secure process.
  • Do not publish home addresses, phone numbers, number plates, medical information or unverified accusations on Facebook.
  • Community-post volume is not a crime-rate measure and should not be used to label Point Vernon unsafe.
