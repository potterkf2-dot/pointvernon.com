Planning an accessible Point Vernon visit

Last checked: 16/08/2026

A paved path or accessible park facility does not make every part of the foreshore step free. Start with documented facilities and confirm the route that matters to you.

  • Best documented startESA Park
  • Beach accessMay include sand, rock or uneven surfaces

Documented public facilities

ESA Park is the clearest starting point

The Queensland accessible tourism guide records designated accessible parking, accessible picnic tables and an accessible toilet at ESA Park, between Aplin and Flinders Streets.

The beach is a separate decision

The same guide describes beach access by a short but sandy path. That means the park facilities can be accessible while the sand and intertidal shoreline may not suit every wheelchair, mobility aid or visitor.

Check the current Council park record and any temporary closure notice before relying on a facility.

Surfaces change by section

Do not label the whole foreshore accessible

Point Vernon includes formed paths, driveway and road crossings, park approaches, sand, roots, rock platforms and tidal flats. A photograph or map line cannot confirm kerb ramps, gradients, passing width, temporary works or the firmness of a surface.

  • Choose a short out-and-back section before committing to a longer route.
  • Keep shoreline exploration separate from travel on the formed path.
  • Confirm shade, seating and an accessible return—not only the outward route.
  • Use a companion or support plan appropriate to the visitor’s needs.

Book for individual needs

Transport without a car

Translink’s Hervey Bay on-demand service includes Point Vernon. Explain mobility equipment or boarding needs when booking and confirm that the offered vehicle and pickup place suit the passenger. Book the return journey as well as the outward trip.
