Primary-record history

Charles Polson, Pialba and Polson Cemetery

Sources checked: 16/08/2026

Community retellings connect Charles Polson with Point Vernon. Queensland’s official records preserve the core story—but they also correct which present-day place once carried the name Polson.

  • Death25 April 1870
  • Burial4 June 1887
  • Name correctionPolson became Pialba, not Point Vernon

Queensland death index

Charles Polson died in 1870

Queensland Births, Deaths and Marriages’ historical index records Charles Polson’s death event on 25 April 1870. The registration is 1870/C/650.

This official index establishes the date. It does not, by itself, establish the exact place or circumstances of his death.

The handwritten cemetery register

“Removed from the Beach” in 1887

Queensland State Archives holds the surviving Polson Cemetery burial register. Its first entry records Charles Polson as a Church of England bank manager, aged about 30, with late residence given as Polson.

The register places him in portion B, grave 1, and gives 4 June 1887 as the burial date. On the facing entry, the handwritten note says “Removed from the Beach”. The register describes the grave as private and lists consumption as the supposed cause of death.

What this proves

The primary record verifies that the register records Charles Polson’s burial at Polson Cemetery on 4 June 1887 with the note “Removed from the Beach”. It does not identify the exact beach location or describe the original burial in more detail.

An important place-name correction

Polson became Pialba—not Point Vernon

Queensland’s official place-name register says the town name Polson was changed to Pialba by Executive Council decision on 19 March 1931. The evidence therefore does not support saying that the suburb of Point Vernon used to be called Polson or Poulsen.

The same register has separate records for the geographic point and the suburb of Point Vernon. It says Maryborough Harbour Master R. B. Sheridan named the point in 1861 after Captain Charles Vernon, commander of HMS Cordelia, which had conveyed Queensland’s first Governor, George Bowen, in 1859. The suburb was named in 1980 after the point.

Careful dating

When was Polson Cemetery operating?

The State Archives catalogue dates the Polson Cemetery Trust from 26 November 1884. The surviving burial register begins on 4 June 1887 with Charles Polson’s entry.

Those records support saying that the cemetery was operating by June 1887. They do not prove that 1887 was its legal opening year; a reserve proclamation or trust instrument would be needed for that more specific claim.

Polson Cemetery remains a place of burial and remembrance. Visitors should enter quietly, avoid touching memorials and give mourners privacy.

Evidence boundary

What remains unverified?

  • The exact place and circumstances of Charles Polson’s death.
  • The location of his original beach grave.
  • Claims about his land ownership or a direct naming link between him and Point Vernon.
  • The later story that two girls were also moved from beach graves.
  • The exact legal opening date of Polson Cemetery.

These details remain research leads, not facts for publication. A newspaper report, land record, reserve notice, trust correspondence or another primary register could resolve them.
