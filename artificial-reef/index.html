A new marine habitat

What is the Point Vernon Artificial Reef?

Queensland Parks says 88 bombora reef structures were placed offshore from Point Vernon in 2025 as part of a Queensland Government artificial-reef program.

Each bombora is a purpose-built concrete module with openings of different sizes. The arrangement is intended to create shelter, shaded surfaces and connected habitat that marine organisms can colonise over time. It is not a shipwreck and it did not exist before the 2025 installation.

Navigate from the authority’s map

Where is the reef?

The official description places it about one nautical mile from Gatakers boat ramp in water approximately 6–8 metres deep.

That description is useful for orientation, not navigation. Use the current Queensland Parks locality map or marine-park mapping tools for the position. Do not estimate a course from a website paragraph, a social-media marker or a shoreline sightline.

A common source of confusion

The artificial reef, natural reef and no-anchoring areas are different

Artificial reef

The 2025 offshore installation of 88 concrete modules described on this page.

Natural shoreline reef

The rock and reef habitat visible around parts of Point Vernon and The Gables, especially at lower tide.

No-anchoring areas

Designated areas at Gatakers Bay and Gables Point shown on marine-park maps. Their rules should not be explained as if they apply only to the artificial reef.

Check every trip

Fishing, diving and marine-park rules

The Queensland Government groups Point Vernon with the Great Sandy Marine Park’s artificial reefs for fishing and diving, but that does not remove zoning, fisheries or vessel-safety requirements. Check the exact zone, designated areas, current fishing limits, closures and protected-species rules before leaving.

Never transfer a rule from a Facebook comment or old screenshot into your trip plan. The official zone map, provisions table and current Queensland recreational-fishing rules are the authorities.

Offshore means preparation

Before travelling to the reef

A map pin is not a safety assessment

  • Check current marine weather, tide, warnings and local notices.
  • Use suitable navigation equipment and know your vessel’s limits.
  • Keep a proper lookout for vessels, fishing gear and people in the water.
  • Log on and off with Marine Rescue Queensland when appropriate for your trip.
  • In a marine emergency, use VHF channel 16 or call Triple Zero (000).
