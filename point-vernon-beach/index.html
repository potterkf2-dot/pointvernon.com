Point Vernon beach

A name within a suburb

Is Point Vernon a suburb or a beach?

Point Vernon is a suburb of Hervey Bay. Beachsafe also uses the name “Point Vernon” for an approximately 1.5-kilometre beach section on the suburb’s northern and eastern shoreline.

Keeping those meanings separate avoids describing the entire suburb as one beach—or assuming every Point Vernon beach reference means Gatakers Bay.

Between two named sections

Where is the beach record?

Beachsafe places this section between the suburb’s northernmost shoreline and Gables Point. Gatakers Bay lies to the west; the longer Gables Point beach continues around the southern side.

The record describes a narrow sandy high-tide beach, broad tidal and linear rock flats, and a continuous foreshore reserve backed by the Esplanade.

Use a named access point

Access, parking and facilities

Beachsafe lists formal parking, drinking water, toilets and a boat ramp within the overall beach record. Those facilities are not necessarily grouped at every access point along 1.5 kilometres of coast.

Council separately names foreshore parks and street-bounded shoreline sections. Choose a signed park or street access, then check that exact place for parking, toilets, shade and ramp restrictions rather than transferring a facility list to the whole beach.

The visible coast expands and contracts

What does the tide change?

At lower water, much more of the rock flat can be exposed and the waterline may be a considerable walk from the high-tide beach. At higher water, the strip of dry sand can narrow.

Plan the return as well as the outward walk. Wet rock, shells and shallow channels can make the most direct route uncomfortable or slippery.

Unpatrolled water

Swimming and shoreline safety

There are no red-and-yellow flags here

Beachsafe records no Surf Life Saving patrol service for Point Vernon beach. Its general hazard rating is not a day-specific safety assessment.

  • Observe depth, wind, water movement and visibility when you arrive.
  • Keep children within reach in and beside the water.
  • Wear secure footwear on rock and shell.
  • Keep clear of boat ramps and marked approaches.
  • If you cannot judge the conditions confidently, stay out of the water.

Why people may use another name

Beach name, park name and street name can differ

A local may describe this shoreline by a nearby park or street rather than say “Point Vernon beach.” Council’s coastline material often identifies management sections by their bounding streets.

The Beachsafe name identifies the beach record; it should not be used to erase more precise local access names. This guide will add those names only when they can be matched to signs, maps or attributed local knowledge.
