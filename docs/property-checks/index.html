Buying in Point Vernon: property checks

Last updated: 4 October 2026

A suburb-wide opinion cannot answer a property-specific question. See the lot, its approvals, services, overlays, title documents and physical condition before committing.

Use this checklist to organise questions and records for the property you are considering. A solicitor and suitable building, planning or insurance professionals can help you interpret the findings before you commit.

Search the street address

Begin with Council’s Online Mapping Index

Council’s mapping portal brings together property, planning, flood, storm-tide, coastal, disaster, infrastructure, bin and other layers. Search the actual address and read the map’s legend, notes and date.

Treat each property separately. A clear map overlay does not rule out every risk or constraint.

Open Council’s Online Mapping Index

What can be built and what was approved?

See planning controls and development records

  • Confirm the zone and all relevant overlays.
  • Search Planning & Development Online for applications, approvals and available documents.
  • See whether existing extensions, sheds, decks, pools or secondary dwellings have the approvals you expect.
  • Review nearby development applications when future construction or land use matters to you.
  • Ask a planner, building certifier, solicitor or surveyor to interpret anything material to your decision.

Property-specific coastal research

Flood, storm tide, erosion and drainage

Point Vernon is a coastal suburb, but exposure varies across individual lots. Review Council’s current flood-hazard information and mapping together with storm-tide, erosion, drainage and evacuation layers where relevant.

Read the mapping alongside site levels, drainage inspections, insurance enquiries and professional assessment. Ask the insurer about the actual address before contracts become unconditional.

Queensland law changed in 2025

Understand the seller disclosure scheme

Since 1 August 2025, Queensland’s mandatory seller disclosure scheme requires a seller to give a buyer specified key property information before the contract is signed. The scheme covers residential and commercial property and vacant land, with exceptions for some transactions.

The Queensland Government says the disclosure statement does not have to include the building’s structural condition, its flood history or previous building and development approvals. Those need separate investigation. Read the official disclosure overview and ask your solicitor which searches and inspections fit the property.

Maps cannot show everything

Inspect the land, building and street

Drainage

Look for site fall, low points, retaining walls, discharge paths, ponding signs and neighbouring runoff.

Coastal wear

Have a building professional assess corrosion, exposed metal, roof fixings, moisture and maintenance—not just cosmetic appearance.

Boundaries

Fences may differ from title boundaries. A survey may be needed for boundaries, encroachments or planned work.

Different conditions

Visit in daylight and after rain if possible. Observe traffic, lighting, access, wind and noise without turning one visit into a universal claim.

Test everyday assumptions

Services and daily life

See the property’s actual water, sewer, stormwater, power, telecommunications, waste collection and access arrangements. Confirm travel to shops, health care, schools or work rather than relying on a suburb description.

For transport without a car, all of Point Vernon is currently in Translink’s bookable on-demand roam zone, but the service has set operating hours and must be reserved. Route 765 is a school service only. Use the transport guide to review booking and operating details, and the moving guide for schools, childcare and electricity setup.

Before committing

Keep a record of your buyer checks

For each item, note the source or document, the date checked, any unresolved question and who will answer it. This makes it easier to compare properties and give your advisers the same information.

  1. Search the address in Council maps and planning records.
  2. Review title, easements, disclosure documents and additional searches with a solicitor.
  3. Obtain suitable building, pest and any specialist inspections.
  4. Ask an insurer about cover and cost for the address.
  5. Confirm approvals and boundaries for structures important to you.
  6. Inspect drainage, access and the street under realistic conditions.
  7. Verify services, transport and travel times.
  8. Write contract conditions with professional advice.
