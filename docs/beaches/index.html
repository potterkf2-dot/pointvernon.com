Choose a stretch of coast

Gatakers Bay

A narrow strip of sand and broad rock shelves beside a working boat ramp. Choose the reserve for bay views and lower water for seeing the shape of the flats.

Explore Gatakers Bay

ESA Park foreshore

A park base for play and a picnic, with the rocky shoreline beside it. Keep beach exploring separate from time in the park.

Explore ESA Park foreshore

The Gables

A foreshore picnic stop beside the site of an old boarding house and the tidal remains of Dunn’s Enclosure. Pair a short shore walk with the history guide.

Explore The Gables

Eli Creek

Mangroves and ridged tidal flats beside a creek mouth. View the habitat from firm public ground and leave the soft flats and creek channels undisturbed.

Explore Eli Creek

Swimming and rock pools

These beaches are unpatrolled. Check the tide before walking onto the rock flats; keep a clear route back and stay clear of boat ramps.

For swimming between red-and-yellow flags, use Beachsafe’s patrolled beach finder. Leave living animals, shells and rocks in their habitat.

Common questions

Is Gatakers Bay the only beach here?

No. Point Vernon also has the Eli Creek shoreline, the foreshore beside ESA Park and The Gables, and other Esplanade access points.

When are the rock pools visible?

Lower water reveals more rock and pools. Use the tide guide to plan around the rising water.

Where should a family start?

Choose a park base such as ESA Park, then add a short foreshore look to suit your group.
