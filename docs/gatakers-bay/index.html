Gatakers Bay, Point Vernon

Last updated: 4 October 2026

Gatakers Bay is the place many people mean when they talk about Point Vernon: a narrow beach and wide rock flats beside a foreshore reserve and public boat ramp.

  • BeachAbout 800 metres long
  • PatrolsNo Surf Life Saving patrol service

A narrow beach and broad rock flats

Gatakers Bay curves between Eli Point and Point Vernon’s northern shoreline. A narrow sandy beach sits above broad rock flats, with reserve and road behind it. The working boat ramp is approached from Corfield Street.

Start with the view from the reserve, then take a short return walk beside the bay. The natural reef and coastal wildlife give this rocky shoreline its character.

Choose your stopping place

Find the bay and nearby parks before setting out. Keep shore-side picnics and walking clear of reversing trailers and launching boats. For a playground or picnic facilities, use ESA Park as a separate stop.

Lower and higher water

Lower tide

Rock shelves spread out from the beach and shallow pools become visible. Secure footwear helps on wet rock and shell.

Higher tide

Water covers the flats and leaves less dry sand. The outlook becomes more water than rock.

These beaches are unpatrolled. Check the tide before walking onto the rock flats; keep a clear route back and stay clear of boat ramps.

The boat ramp

Compare Gatakers Bay with The Gables ramp for lanes, facilities and tide restrictions. The fishing guide links to marine-park zones and no-anchoring areas.

Common questions

Is Gatakers Bay patrolled?

No. There is no Surf Life Saving patrol service.

Where is the boat ramp?

On the foreshore at the Corfield Street approach, separate from the ramp at Aplin Street.

When can I see the rock flats?

Lower water exposes more of the shelves. The tide guide links to predictions for planning your visit.
