ESA Park Playground, Point Vernon

Last updated: 12 November 2026

Plan an ESA Park visit on the Point Vernon Esplanade. Find the playground, picnic shelters, barbecues, toilets, parking and nearby foreshore walks.

Getting there and parking

ESA Park is on the Esplanade between Aplin and Flinders Streets in Point Vernon. It combines a playground and picnic facilities with access to the foreshore, so it makes a useful first stop when your group wants more than a beach walk. Find ESA Park on the local map and open directions to the named park.

There is off-street parking. Choose the park as your destination rather than a general Point Vernon search: the suburb has several separate parks and boat-ramp entrances along its shoreline. Once you arrive, locate the toilets and the path you want to use before unloading picnic gear. If someone in your group needs a particular parking arrangement or a short, step-free approach, check that detail before travelling.

Playground time and a picnic

The playground sits back from the road. The July 2024 photograph shows swings and climbing equipment beneath shade sails. Keep the age and confidence of each child in mind when choosing equipment, and supervise play throughout the visit. A photograph can help you recognise the park; check the equipment and surroundings when you arrive.

Picnic shelters, barbecues, seating, showers and a water tap make ESA Park a practical base for a longer pause. Bring your own food, drinking water, sun protection and picnic supplies. For a group meal, decide whether you need a table or can use a picnic rug, and have a simple alternative if your preferred shelter is occupied. The facilities listing does not reserve a space for your visit.

You can make the playground the whole outing, or leave the picnic gear packed until everyone has had a short walk. Keeping those plans flexible is useful with young children: there is no need to cover the whole foreshore to enjoy a stop here. For other options, compare Point Vernon parks and playgrounds.

The shore changes with the tide

The beach and tidal rock pools are beside the park, and the amount of exposed shore changes through the day. Decide whether you want to stay in the park and on the foreshore path or make the shore part of the visit. Read the tide and weather guide before planning around exposed rock pools.

Point Vernon beaches are unpatrolled. A playground visit does not need to include entering the water. For a shore walk, check the conditions in front of you and keep a clear way back. The beach guide explains the different shoreline areas if swimming or a longer beach outing is your main reason for coming.

A turtle rookery lies beside ESA Park. The park has warm, shielded lighting designed to reduce light reaching the beach and disorientating hatchlings. Keep the beach dark when visiting after sunset and give wildlife space. Enjoying the park during daylight is an easy way to combine play, a picnic and a look at the coast.

Toilets, access and dogs

The park has toilets, including an all-abilities toilet. That is a useful starting point for planning, but a toilet listing does not describe every gradient, crossing or approach. Use the accessibility guide and check any detail essential to your visit, such as the route between your vehicle, the toilet and the playground.

For a visit with a dog, read the foreshore dog guide before choosing your walking area. Playground exclusions and signed boundaries matter; a nearby off-leash area does not extend across the whole park. Plan where each person and the dog will spend time if children also want to use the playground.

Turn one stop into a longer outing

Stay on the Esplanade for a short foreshore walk, then return to your picnic base. Choose your turnaround around your group’s energy and the weather rather than treating a longer route as compulsory. The walking guide has options for a short stroll and a defined 5 km return.

For a different coastal stop, compare Gatakers Bay and The Gables before setting out. If play equipment and confirmed toilets are the priorities, keep ESA Park as your main base. Travelling with children? The Hervey Bay Kids ESA Park guide provides another family planning route.
