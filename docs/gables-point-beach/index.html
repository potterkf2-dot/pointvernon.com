Beach and park: two different scales

Beachsafe’s Gables Point beach record describes about 3.5 km of south-facing shore, backed by The Esplanade and a foreshore reserve. A steep, narrow strip of high-tide sand fronts linear rock flats roughly 50–100 m wide. It is a different named section from the east-facing Point Vernon beach.

The Gables park is a smaller destination within the wider foreshore. Choose the park for its picnic facilities and the wider shore for coastal views. The 3.5 km figure describes the shoreline; use the walks guide for path distances and the map to choose a stop.

Plan a picnic at The Gables

For a relaxed shore-side stop, find The Gables park and make the park your base. Council’s park record lists barbecues, picnic shelters, seating, a water tap and a dog-bag dispenser. It does not list toilets. If toilets are essential for your outing, choose a separate park with listed facilities before you leave.

Off-leash permission depends on the signs for the exact section, even where dog bags are provided. Use the dog-walking guide to plan your visit. For a step-free outing, use the accessibility guide to choose a suitable route. The boat-ramp guide covers the separate boating destinations; keep pedestrian stops clear of ramp operations.

A shore shaped by the tide

The rock platform becomes more prominent as the water falls; higher water leaves less dry beach. The tidal range is large and the beach is unpatrolled. Enjoy the view from a suitable public access point; for swimming, choose a patrolled beach and stay between the red and yellow flags.

Check tides and weather, keep a clear way back and avoid climbing on wet or uneven rock. Queensland’s rocky-shore advice asks visitors to leave rocks and shells in place, avoid trampling marine life and approach from beaches or formed stairways instead of cutting down banks.

Add the local history

The park name also leads to the story of The Gables boarding house and Dunn’s Enclosure. Read the history guide before your visit to put the shoreline remains in context. View them from a comfortable distance and leave the stones undisturbed.
