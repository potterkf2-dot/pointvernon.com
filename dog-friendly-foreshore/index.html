Dog-friendly Point Vernon foreshore

Last checked: 14/08/2026

Point Vernon has several 24-hour off-leash foreshore sections, but not every beach or playground approach is off leash. The signs at your access point are the final word.

  • Current ruleOff leash only in signed areas
  • ControlReliable recall is required
  • ChangesCouncil rules are being updated during 2026

Published Council boundaries

Where are the off-leash sections?

Gatakers Bay towards ESA Park

Council lists a 24-hour off-leash section from beach marker PVT20, about 300 metres west of Gatakers boat ramp, to 100 metres west of the ESA Park playground, as signed.

Aplin Street towards The Pines

Council lists a 24-hour off-leash section between Aplin Street and The Pines Park. A playground-related exception runs from the southern entry to 40 metres north of the last playground. Follow the signs.

Western foreshore towards Dundowran Beach

Council lists the shoreline from Ansons Road to the end of Spence Street, Point Vernon, as a 24-hour off-leash area.

Do not use this page as a boundary map

Beach rules can change and the exact transition points are easier to understand on Council’s map and foreshore signs. If an area is not signed off leash or dog free, Council says dogs must be on a leash.

Rules in transition

Why the signs matter in 2026

Council began changing Hervey Bay dog-beach rules in February 2026 and says a second stage is planned later in the year, subject to local-law changes. That stage is expected to add dog-free areas and a seasonal shorebird-protection zone.

Check the current Council map before a new route and obey the newest signs on site, even if an older online post or saved map says something different.

Effective control

Off leash does not mean uncontrolled

  • Release your dog only if it returns reliably when called.
  • Ask before allowing a greeting; some people and dogs need space.
  • Keep close enough to intervene before play becomes rough or intrusive.
  • Carry bags and put droppings in a bin.
  • Leash your dog near traffic, crowded entries, boats, fishing activity or whenever conditions make recall uncertain.
  • Do not let your dog stop other people enjoying the foreshore.

A living coastline

Keep dogs away from wildlife and play areas

Point Vernon and Gatakers Bay include shorebird habitat and tidal flats. Give birds a wide berth and leash your dog before it can chase, flush or corner wildlife.

Playground exceptions are part of the signed boundaries. Children may be frightened even by a friendly dog, so slow down and use a leash through busy entries and family areas.

When something goes wrong

Reporting a dog attack or urgent risk

Council asks people to report a dog attack as soon as possible on 1300 79 49 29, which it lists as available 24 hours for emergencies, or through its online reporting form. Seek medical or veterinary care first when needed and safely preserve notes, photographs and witness details.
