Point Vernon history

First story

Butchulla Country

Point Vernon is on Butchulla Country. Butchulla connections extend across the adjacent mainland and K’gari, formerly known as Fraser Island, and through the waters and living systems of the Great Sandy region.

This page is only a settler-written introduction. Butchulla history is not a preface to later development; it is continuing culture, knowledge and responsibility. Readers should prefer material led or approved by Butchulla people when learning about language, law and cultural places.

A mapped name

How Point Vernon received its colonial name

Queensland Places records the name Point Vernon in 1861 and links it to Captain Charles Egerton Harcourt-Vernon, a naval officer associated with surveying in the area. The modern suburb grew much later around the low headland on Hervey Bay’s northern edge.

Place names hold only one layer of history. A colonial map label should not be mistaken for the beginning of the place or its story.

Early seaside life

The Gables and Bayview Boarding House

By the late nineteenth century, the foreshore was being promoted as a place for seaside recreation. Council’s heritage record says Bayview Boarding House was established at The Gables in 1888. It served visitors before road travel and modern holiday accommodation reshaped Hervey Bay tourism.

The Gables remains a public foreshore place. Although the boarding house does not survive as a visitor attraction, the outlook helps explain why people gathered here.

Shoreline structure

Dunn’s Enclosure and the earlier fish trap

Low stone remains near The Gables are entered in Council’s local heritage record as Dunn’s Enclosure and Fish Trap. The record says a fish trap is believed to have been associated with the Corbet family, while Andrew Dunn built a swimming enclosure around the 1920s after a fatal shark attack in 1922. Community working bees repaired or extended the enclosure in the 1930s.

The structure is significant as a surviving example of a seaside bathing enclosure and as evidence of changing attitudes to recreation and safety. The two histories—the earlier fish trap and later swimming enclosure—should not be collapsed into one use.

View, do not climb

These are tidal remains in an active marine environment. Avoid moving stone, standing on unstable sections or handling marine life. Check the tide and use secure footwear.

Community memory

Polson Cemetery

Polson Cemetery is a small cemetery in Point Vernon. Fraser Coast Regional Council notes that it includes a South Sea Islander Memorial and Cemetery, recognising a community whose labour and lives are central to the region’s history.

A cemetery is not a conventional attraction. If you visit, enter quietly, keep to paths where possible, do not touch memorials, and allow private mourners space.

Read the landscape

Visit heritage places with care

  • Use public access and follow every current sign.
  • Do not remove stone, shell, plants or artefacts.
  • Keep exact locations of sensitive cultural material private.
  • Check Council’s heritage place cards before interpreting a site.
  • Report damage to the relevant land manager rather than trying to repair it.
