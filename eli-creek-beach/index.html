Northwestern shoreline

Eli Creek beach, Point Vernon

Last checked: 14/08/2026

Eli Creek is the short, north-facing beach section beside the creek mouth and Eli Point—not another name for Gatakers Bay.

One of four beach records

Which part of the coast is Eli Creek beach?

Beachsafe uses “Eli Creek” for the beach between the small Eli Creek mouth and Eli Point. Gatakers Bay begins on the other side of Eli Point, so the two are separate catalogue entries.

The name is most useful for identifying the creek-edge stretch. Locals may also describe a visit by the nearby reserve, road or access point.

A different coastal setting

What is the shoreline like?

Beachsafe describes a north-facing strip of high-tide sand, scattered mangroves and extensive ridged tidal flats. The visual experience changes markedly with the tide: lower water exposes a much larger intertidal area, while higher water compresses the usable shoreline.

This is a creek-and-tidal-flat environment, not a conventional open sandy swimming beach.

Plan the access point

Parking and access context

Beachsafe lists a formal parking area for the beach record but does not provide a street-by-street access guide. Council places Parraweena Park Bushland Reserve between the Eli Creek mouth and Eli Point, with the reserve entrance at the corner of Spence and Corser Streets.

Use current signs and Council mapping when choosing where to park. Do not block a residential access, drive onto vegetation or assume a visible track is a public vehicle route.

Creek mouths need caution

Tide, soft ground and moving water

Do not read “low energy” as “always safe”

Creek mouths can have moving water, channels, mud or soft sediment. A tide prediction cannot show the firmness of the ground or the exact route water will take on the day.

  • Check current tide and weather before going onto the flats.
  • Keep children within reach near channels and the creek mouth.
  • Turn back before a rising tide narrows the return route.
  • Give mangroves, birds and other wildlife plenty of space.
  • Choose secure footwear rather than assuming the whole section is soft sand.

Landward neighbour

Parraweena Park Bushland Reserve

Council records Parraweena Park between Eli Creek and Eli Point. The reserve preserves remnant eucalypt forest and the story of the Cook family’s former bird sanctuary and land donation.

It is a separate bushland destination with ecological and historical value; visit the dedicated guide rather than treating it simply as beach parking.
