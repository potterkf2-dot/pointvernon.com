Six named places to find

Choose the named place that suits your outing, open its map, then check the guide for facilities. The Google Maps links are searches for Point Vernon, Queensland. Confirm the correct result before selecting directions, and save the meeting place and return route if travelling with others.

Gatakers Bay boat ramp

A landmark for finding Gatakers Bay and the nearby foreshore. Use the bay guide to plan a shoreline stop; the ramp itself is a working boating area, so choose a meeting or viewing place clear of boat and vehicle movements.

ESA Park

Choose ESA Park for a playground and picnic stop. Council lists picnic shelters, barbecues and toilets; the park guide explains facilities and links to accessibility information.

The Gables park

A foreshore picnic stop with a local history story. This link searches for the park, which is only one part of the longer beach section called Gables Point.

Parraweena Park

Explore the bushland setting and history of the former bird sanctuary. Council lists this reserve as open space; read the guide before relying on picnic or playground facilities.

The Gables boat ramp

The working ramp approached from Aplin Street is a separate destination from The Gables park. Council lists one lane and a low-tide restriction. Use the ramp comparison to plan launching and retrieval.

Webb Park

A neighbourhood play stop on Murphy Street. Council lists a playground, basketball, seating and shelter, but does not list toilets. Bring a ball if basketball is part of your plan.

What do you need to find?

Toilets, a picnic or play

Use the parks comparison and the exact Council park record. Choose a specific park with the facilities you need; a facility listed for a long beach record may not be present at every access.

Walking or cycling

Use the walks guide, then inspect the section for surfaces, crossings, temporary works and a comfortable return.

Public transport

Point Vernon is covered by a bookable service. Read the getting-around guide and confirm the journey with Translink.

A route that suits your mobility

Use the accessibility checklist to check the parking bay, entrance, path and toilet as one journey. A map line alone cannot establish that the whole route is step free.

Check the access point when you arrive

Maps are planning tools, not guarantees

A map cannot confirm that a path is clear, a toilet is open, a parking space is available, a ramp suits a vessel or an intertidal route is safe. Follow current signs and turn back when the place does not match the plan.

  • Do not block driveways or use an unsigned vehicle track.
  • Keep out of boat-ramp approaches and marked channels.
  • Check the tide before walking onto exposed rock or sand flats.
  • Check the dog access guide and follow the latest signed boundaries when you arrive.
