Point Vernon parks and playgrounds

Last updated: 8 September 2026

By Point Vernon Guide · How we check this guide

Point Vernon has foreshore parks for picnics and play, plus smaller neighbourhood and bushland spaces. Facilities differ, so choose the park for what you actually need.

Need a toilet and playground together? Start with ESA Park. For a picnic shelter and barbecue, compare ESA Park with The Gables. Council does not list a toilet at The Gables, Webb Park, Parraweena Park or Ian Drewe Memorial Park; confirm separately if one is essential.

Foreshore facilities

ESA Park: playground, toilets and picnic facilities

ESA Park sits on the Esplanade between Aplin and Flinders streets. Council lists a playground, picnic and barbecue facilities, toilets, showers, water and seating. It also describes turtle-friendly lighting intended to reduce light spill towards an adjacent nesting area.

Find ESA Park on the map and check the ESA Park accessibility notes if a particular facility is essential. The adjacent beach remains unpatrolled and the rock pools need close supervision.

Picnic with a view

The Gables: a foreshore picnic and local history

Council lists a barbecue, picnic shelter, seating, water tap and dog-bag dispenser at The Gables. The site also has historical interest: remains of Dunn’s Enclosure and an earlier fish trap lie on this part of the foreshore.

Find The Gables on the map and read the boarding house and Dunn’s Enclosure history to add context to a picnic or shoreline walk. The nearby Aplin Street boat ramp is listed as tide restricted. Keep picnic and play activity clear of vehicles and boats using the ramp.

Away from the water

Neighbourhood and nature parks

Webb Park

On Murphy Street, where Council lists a playground, seating, basketball facility and shelter. No toilets or barbecue are listed.

Parraweena Park

A 5.8-hectare bushland reserve on the western side of Point Vernon, containing remnant eucalypt forest and traces of the former Parraweena Bird Sanctuary. Treat it as quiet open space rather than a serviced playground.

Read the sanctuary history and reserve guide

Ian Drewe Memorial Park

Open space on Banksia Street suited to a simple kick of a ball or short local stop. Council does not list toilets, shelter or play equipment.

Council facility records can change after repairs or upgrades. Confirm shade, fencing, accessible parking and current works if any feature is essential to your visit.

Practical cautions

Access, shade and the shoreline

“All-abilities toilet” describes a listed facility, not the entire journey from parking space to playground, table or beach. Kerbs, path surfaces, slopes and temporary works still need an on-site check.

The accessibility guide separates Council-verified ESA Park facilities from the beach’s sandy access limitation and lists useful questions to confirm before travelling.

  • Bring sun protection even when a shelter is listed; playground shade can be limited.
  • Check equipment temperature before children use it.
  • Keep young children within reach near the unpatrolled foreshore.
  • Read current dog and wildlife signs; off-leash boundaries do not automatically include playgrounds.
