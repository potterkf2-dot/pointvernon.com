Move beside the water

Point Vernon walks and cycling

The foreshore path is the simplest way to understand Point Vernon. Take a short out-and-back walk, link the western and eastern sides, or continue towards Pialba.

  • StyleMostly choose-your-own-distance foreshore travel
  • UsersWalkers, cyclists, children and dogs share sections
  • CheckSurface, crossings and access before relying on a route

Route ideas

Three useful ways to follow the foreshore

Council mapping shows a pedestrian path running along the Esplanade around Point Vernon and connections towards Pialba. The route works better as a series of sections than as one compulsory circuit.

Routes are intentionally flexible

Path continuity, crossings, surfaces and temporary works can change. Choose a short out-and-back section first, then extend it only when the route ahead suits your group.

Before relying on the route

Surfaces, prams and mobility access

Do not assume the entire foreshore has the same surface or width. A paved section can still include driveway crossings, road crossings, sand, roots, maintenance work or short missing links.

Access details vary by section

  • Look ahead for gaps in paving, sand, roots and temporary works.
  • Check kerb ramps, crossing points and gradients before committing to a longer route.
  • Match the chosen section to the wheelchair, mobility scooter or pram being used.
  • Confirm accessible parking and toilet locations through current Council information.

Until those details are checked, the guide should not label a route “wheelchair accessible” simply because a park or short path section appears paved.

Shared space

Cycling without rushing the path

Point Vernon’s foreshore is better suited to a relaxed ride than speed. Expect people to stop for views, children to change direction, dogs on leads or in signed off-leash areas, and sand or leaves on the surface.

  • Slow down before passing and use a bell or calm voice early.
  • Give pedestrians a wide berth and be ready to stop.
  • Use lights in low visibility, but avoid dazzling other path users or wildlife.
  • Dismount where signs, congestion or a narrow section make riding unsafe.

A comfortable outing

Weather, tide and dogs

There is limited shade on exposed shoreline sections, and wind can make a return leg feel very different from the outward trip. Carry water, use sun protection and check the forecast before committing to a longer walk.

The tide matters if you plan to leave the formed path and look at the rock flats. Keep walking and rock-pool exploring as separate decisions: a safe paved route does not make the adjacent intertidal rocks safe.

Council lists specific signed Point Vernon foreshore sections as off leash. Read the boundary signs at the place you enter, keep dogs under effective control and avoid wildlife areas.
