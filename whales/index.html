Whale watching from Point Vernon

Last updated: 14/08/2026

When whales enter the bay

Hervey Bay whale season

Queensland Government guidance says about a quarter of the east-coast humpback population enters Hervey Bay on the southward migration, creating a distinctive viewing opportunity between August and October. Great Sandy Marine Park information describes most sightings as occurring on the eastern side of the bay, close to K’gari, generally from July to October.

K’gari is the island’s Butchulla name; it was formerly known as Fraser Island. Its position shelters Hervey Bay and helps create the resting area used by humpbacks, including mothers and calves.

From Point Vernon

Where to try whale watching from land

Point Vernon has broad water views, but official sources do not identify it as a guaranteed or consistently close land-based whale viewpoint. Think of whale spotting as something to add to a foreshore visit, not the sole reason to make a long trip.

The northern foreshore

Open views across the bay and room to scan a wide horizon. Choose a safe position back from wet rock and any unstable edge.

The Gables foreshore

South and east-facing water views from park and foreshore areas. Trees, tide and local structures may affect the clearest line of sight.

Gatakers Bay side

A pleasant place to watch the bay, although the western orientation is not necessarily where most Hervey Bay whales will be seen.

No guaranteed shore viewpoint

Choose any safe public foreshore position with a broad horizon and comfortable access. Sightings from land are opportunistic, and no single Point Vernon location can promise a better result on a particular day.

Watch the water

How to improve your chances

Calm water makes blows, backs and splashes easier to distinguish. Queensland guidance says mornings are often calmer before wind increases, while low late-afternoon light can also help show a blow or breach.

  1. Use binoculars. Scan slowly rather than staring at one patch of water.
  2. Look for a blow first. A brief puff above the horizon is often easier to notice than the animal’s back.
  3. Watch the same area again. A travelling whale may surface several times before a longer dive.
  4. Give it time. A quiet half-hour can still produce no sighting; that is normal.

Never climb onto unsafe rock or distract a driver because someone has reported a whale. Use formed public areas and keep children back from edges.

A closer view

When a boat tour makes sense

On-water tours travel towards the parts of Hervey Bay where whales are more often encountered, so they offer a much better chance of a sighting than scanning from Point Vernon. Tours depart from the Urangan harbour area, not Point Vernon.

For a neutral overview of current operators and accommodation, use the independent Visit Hervey Bay guide. Check each operator’s current season, departure point, accessibility and cancellation terms directly.

Quick answers

Common whale-watching questions

What month is best for whales in Hervey Bay?

Official Queensland guidance centres the Hervey Bay opportunity on August to October. Operators may run across a wider July-to-October window depending on annual movement and conditions.

Can you see whales from Point Vernon?

It is possible to spot whales from Queensland headlands and beaches, but a Point Vernon sighting is not guaranteed and whales in Hervey Bay are commonly farther east near K’gari.

What should I bring?

Binoculars, sun protection, water and patience. A camera with optical zoom can help, but do not let photography draw you towards an unsafe edge.

Are mornings or afternoons better?

Calm conditions are most helpful. Queensland guidance notes that mornings are often calmer, while late-afternoon light can make blows and breaches easier to see.
