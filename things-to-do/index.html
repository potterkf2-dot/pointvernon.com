Things to do in Point Vernon

Last updated: 19 September 2026

Follow the foreshore, take a picnic to the park or join the Saturday parkrun. Point Vernon’s pleasures are simple: bay views, a changing tidal shoreline and time outdoors. Here are places to start and ideas for the time you have.

  • Start hereGatakers Bay, ESA Park or the foreshore path
  • Before leavingCheck tide, weather and facilities
  • Water safetyPoint Vernon beaches are unpatrolled

Coast and water

Start with the shoreline

Discover the bay at Gatakers

Watch the boats and the changing shoreline from the reserve beside Gatakers Bay. For a different coastal setting, compare the creek edge at Eli Creek and the rock flats along Point Vernon beach and Gables Point.

Start with Gatakers Bay

Compare Point Vernon beaches

Walk or ride beside the water

Set out from the Corfield Street foreshore or choose an Esplanade section on the eastern side. Start with a short out-and-back wander, allowing time for a view and a rest. Check the surface and crossings before extending your route.

Look closer at Point Vernon’s natural coral reef, mangroves and shorebirds. Find ideas for quiet observation and learn why ESA Park has turtle-friendly lights.

Plan a fishing outing

Compare Gatakers Bay and The Gables boat ramps for facilities, tide restrictions and servicing.

Explore shore-fishing and boating options around the rocky foreshore. The guide explains the two public ramp locations and links to Queensland fishing rules and marine-park zoning.

Learn about the artificial reef

Discover the offshore reef installed in 2025 and how it differs from the natural rock and reef visible along the shoreline. It is an offshore destination, so start with the official locality map and boating information.

Parks, wildlife and history

Picnics, community and local stories

Picnic and play at ESA Park

Pair the playground with a picnic beside the foreshore. Council lists shelters, barbecues and toilets; check the access details and keep children closely supervised near the unpatrolled beach. Plan an ESA Park visit.

Join the Saturday parkrun

Walk, jog, run or volunteer at the free 5 km event, starting at 7:00am opposite 138 The Esplanade. Register once and check the organiser’s page for cancellations. Plan your first parkrun.

Whales from land

Winter and spring provide the main opportunity, but a land sighting is never guaranteed. Read the land-based whale guide.

Local history

Follow the documented stories of The Gables, Dunn’s Enclosure and other local places. Start with the history guide.

Coffee and a foreshore picnic

Find a neighbourhood coffee or takeaway stop, or bring food to a park with picnic facilities. Explore local food and coffee and confirm trading details with the business.

A quiet moment with wildlife

Look for wildlife while enjoying the foreshore, rather than planning around a guaranteed sighting. The owner photographed these kangaroos in Point Vernon in June 2023.

Give wild animals room to move, keep dogs away and never feed them.
