Seaside history

The Gables, Point Vernon

Last checked: 16/08/2026

The name now belongs to a foreshore park and point, but it once referred to a large wooden boarding house built during Hervey Bay’s early years as a seaside destination.

  • Built1888 as Bayview Boarding House
  • Later namesVernon, then The Gables
  • DemolishedAround 1957

An anticipated railway

Why was the boarding house built?

Fraser Coast Libraries records that Mr Antcliff built Bayview Boarding House in 1888. The decision was made on the expectation that the railway would be extended to Point Vernon.

The railway did not turn Point Vernon into the terminus imagined by that investment, but the building became part of Hervey Bay’s early seaside-resort history, when visitors from Maryborough were beginning to stay along the coast.

A name that outlived the building

Bayview, Vernon and The Gables

The same wooden establishment was known first as Bayview Boarding House, later as Vernon and finally as The Gables. The last name endured after the building disappeared and is now used for the nearby foreshore park and Gables Point.

A documented ownership sequence

Owners and additions

1898–1908

Hon. Andrew Heron Wilson and Mrs Wilson owned the property from 10 November 1898 until 23 June 1908. Wilson was associated with large Maryborough sawmills and served in Queensland’s Legislative Council.

1908–1913

Alexander and Louisa Corbet then owned the property until 16 July 1913. Fraser Coast Libraries says they brought horses from Gympie and added stables and coach houses.

From 1914

Mr and Mrs L. M. Upton purchased the property in 1914 and later sold it to Dr Bendeich.

The end of the house

Why was The Gables demolished?

By 1957 the building had deteriorated substantially. Fraser Coast Libraries records that it was sold to Mr R. Dillon of Kingaroy and the decision was made to demolish it.

Historical photographs held or reproduced by local collections show the house and its demolition, but those images are not copied here. A historical image still needs a clear owner, licence and provenance before this website can republish it.

Reading the present place

What is at The Gables today?

Council’s current park record lists picnic shelters, barbecues, seating, a water tap, dog-bag dispensers and a scenic viewpoint. It does not list a toilet at the park.

The shoreline also contains the remains associated with Dunn’s Enclosure, commonly remembered as the Point Vernon rock baths. Council’s heritage record dates construction of that stone enclosure to around the 1920s. It is a separate story from the boarding house, although both are part of the same small area’s seaside history.

What remains open to further research?

This page uses published dates, not Facebook recollection

Community history posts helped identify the topic. The dates and ownership sequence here come from Fraser Coast Libraries and its cited references.

A fuller account could still establish the daily life of guests and workers, the building’s exact changes over time and more first-hand memories. Those details should come from attributed interviews, newspapers, directories, property records and permissioned photographs.
