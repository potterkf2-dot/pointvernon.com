Buying in Point Vernon: property checks

Last checked: 14/08/2026

A suburb-wide opinion cannot answer a property-specific question. Check the lot, its approvals, services, overlays, title documents and physical condition before committing.

General information only

This page is not legal, financial, engineering, surveying, building or insurance advice. Rules and property records change, and official maps have limitations. Engage appropriately qualified professionals for the property and transaction.

Search the street address

Begin with Council’s Online Mapping Index

Council’s mapping portal brings together property, planning, flood, storm-tide, coastal, disaster, infrastructure, bin and other layers. Search the actual address and read the map’s legend, notes and date.

Do not assume a layer affecting one part of Point Vernon applies to every property—or that a property with no obvious coloured overlay has no risk or constraint.

Open Council’s Online Mapping Index

What can be built and what was approved?

Check planning controls and development records

  • Confirm the zone and all relevant overlays.
  • Search Planning & Development Online for applications, approvals and available documents.
  • Check whether existing extensions, sheds, decks, pools or secondary dwellings have the approvals you expect.
  • Review nearby development applications when future construction or land use matters to you.
  • Ask a planner, building certifier, solicitor or surveyor to interpret anything material to your decision.

Property-specific coastal research

Flood, storm tide, erosion and drainage

Point Vernon is a coastal suburb, but exposure varies across individual lots. Review Council’s current flood-hazard information and mapping together with storm-tide, erosion, drainage and evacuation layers where relevant.

A map is a starting point, not a guarantee of the maximum event or a substitute for site levels, drainage inspection, insurance enquiries and professional assessment. Ask the insurer about the actual address before contracts become unconditional.

Queensland law changed in 2025

Understand the seller disclosure scheme

Since 1 August 2025, Queensland’s mandatory seller disclosure scheme requires a seller to give a buyer specified key property information before the contract is signed. The scheme covers residential and commercial property and vacant land.

Disclosure does not replace the buyer’s own searches, physical inspections or legal advice. Ask your solicitor what is included, what is excluded and which additional searches fit the property.

Maps cannot show everything

Inspect the land, building and street

Drainage

Look for site fall, low points, retaining walls, discharge paths, ponding signs and neighbouring runoff.

Coastal wear

Have a building professional assess corrosion, exposed metal, roof fixings, moisture and maintenance—not just cosmetic appearance.

Boundaries

Do not assume fences match the title. A survey may be needed for boundaries, encroachments or planned work.

Different conditions

Visit in daylight and after rain if possible. Observe traffic, lighting, access, wind and noise without turning one visit into a universal claim.

Test everyday assumptions

Services and daily life

Check the property’s actual water, sewer, stormwater, power, telecommunications, waste collection and access arrangements. Confirm travel to shops, health care, schools or work rather than relying on a suburb description.

For transport without a car, all of Point Vernon is currently in Translink’s bookable on-demand roam zone, but the service has set operating hours and must be reserved. Route 765 is a school service only.

Before committing

A concise buyer checklist

  1. Search the address in Council maps and planning records.
  2. Review title, easements, disclosure documents and additional searches with a solicitor.
  3. Obtain suitable building, pest and any specialist inspections.
  4. Check insurance availability and cost for the address.
  5. Confirm approvals and boundaries for structures important to you.
  6. Inspect drainage, access and the street under realistic conditions.
  7. Verify services, transport and travel times.
  8. Write contract conditions with professional advice; do not rely on a social-media reassurance.
