Point Vernon’s western bay

Gatakers Bay, Point Vernon

Last updated: 12/08/2026

Gatakers Bay is the place many people mean when they talk about Point Vernon: a narrow beach and wide rock flats beside a foreshore reserve and public boat ramp.

First impressions

What to expect at Gatakers Bay

The beach curves between Eli Point and the central part of Point Vernon. Beachsafe records a narrow sandy high-tide beach with rock flats extending roughly 100 metres seaward. A reserve and road sit behind the shoreline.

This is not a conventional surf beach. Its character comes from the tide, the rocky intertidal area, boating activity and the wide outlook across Hervey Bay.

Check facilities on arrival

Facilities, parking capacity and construction conditions can change. Use current Council information and signs at the reserve rather than relying on an old directory entry.

Timing matters

Choose the tide for what you want to do

Lower tide

More of the linear rock flats become visible. This suits shoreline observation, but wet rock, shells and uneven footing need care.

Higher tide

Water covers more of the rock platform and the sandy strip becomes narrower. Keep clear of the marked boating channel and ramp activity.

Changing tide

Do not walk a long way onto exposed flats without watching the return route. The tide can cut across low sections before the entire platform is covered.

Enter the water carefully

Swimming and snorkelling at Gatakers Bay

Gatakers Bay is unpatrolled. Tourism material mentions snorkelling around the Point Vernon reef, but visibility, depth, tide, wind and boating traffic vary. Do not read that as a promise that conditions are suitable on a particular day.

Before entering

  • Check the latest weather, tide and marine warnings.
  • Stay away from the ramp, dredged channel and moving boats.
  • Look for sharp or slippery rock under shallow water.
  • Do not snorkel alone, and do not enter if visibility or conditions are poor.

Shared access

Boat ramp and fishing

The state-owned public ramp at Corfield Street is managed by Fraser Coast Regional Council. Council’s register currently lists it separately from the smaller Aplin Street ramp at The Gables.

Point Vernon is surrounded by a Great Sandy Marine Park conservation zone. Line limits apply, and designated no-anchoring areas protect reef habitat near Gatakers Bay and Gables Point. Check the current zoning map and Queensland fishing rules before launching or fishing.

Stay a little longer

Picnics and late-afternoon light

The reserve behind the beach gives the bay a natural pause between water, road and houses. If you visit later in the day, the western side of the headland can catch warm light across the water. The exact sunset position shifts through the year, so think of it as a good place to watch the day change rather than a guaranteed sun-over-water photograph.

Use existing tables and shelters where available, take rubbish with you if bins are full, and avoid lighting or flash near any signed turtle-sensitive area.

Quick answers

Common questions about Gatakers Bay

Can you swim at Gatakers Bay?

People enter the water, but it is an unpatrolled beach with rock flats and boating activity. Assess the conditions on the day and do not enter if you are unsure.

Is the boat ramp tide restricted?

Council’s current register marks the Aplin Street ramp at The Gables as low-tide restricted, but does not mark the Corfield Street Gatakers Bay ramp that way. Local depth and channel conditions still need to be checked before launching.

Can I fish from the shore?

Fishing occurs around Point Vernon, but access, marine zoning and other users must be considered. Check current rules and avoid casting near swimmers, paths or the ramp.

Is Gatakers Bay suitable for young children?

The reserve may suit a family stop, but the beach is unpatrolled and the foreshore includes slippery rock, a boat ramp and moving water. Close supervision is essential.
