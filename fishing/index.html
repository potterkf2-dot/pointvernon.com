Fish the rocky foreshore carefully

Point Vernon fishing

Last updated: 19 September 2026

Point Vernon offers shore and boating access beside rocky habitat and inshore reef. It is part of the Great Sandy Marine Park, so check the zone and any designated-area rules for the exact place you intend to fish.

From the foreshore

Land-based fishing around Point Vernon

Rock platforms, beach edges and the approaches to Gatakers Bay attract fishers, but not every piece of shoreline is a sensible casting position. Check the tide, avoid slippery rock, and leave clear space between hooks and people using the beach or path.

Gatakers Bay edge

A recognised fishing area with a working boat ramp and channel. Fish well clear of launching vessels and never cast across marked access.

Compare the ramp before you launch

The Gatakers Bay and The Gables boat-ramp guide compares lane counts, listed load limits, water and toilets, low-tide restrictions and servicing. Check both the departure and retrieval plan.

Rocky shoreline sections

Structure can attract fish, but wet rock, waves from passing boats and a returning tide create risk. Do not climb down where the route back is uncertain.

The Gables side

A long foreshore with rock flats and a smaller ramp. Check local signs and keep away from swimmers, dogs and ramp traffic.

What may be around

Find out what is realistic for your visit

Decide whether you will fish from the shore or a boat before choosing tackle or following a fishing report. A report from offshore reef grounds may tell you little about what is practical from Point Vernon’s beach edges.

Ask a current local tackle business about realistic shore-based options for the week you visit, then check every species identification, size limit, possession limit and closure against Queensland’s official rules before keeping a fish.

Local advice does not replace the rules

Fishing reports can be useful, but they may describe boat or reef catches rather than the Point Vernon shore. Use official fisheries and marine-park information as the authority.

Installed offshore in 2025

Point Vernon Artificial Reef

Queensland Parks confirms that 88 concrete bombora structures were installed about one nautical mile from Gatakers boat ramp in water approximately 6–8 metres deep. Use the authority’s locality map for the position rather than navigating from that description.

The artificial reef is separate from Point Vernon’s natural shoreline reef and from the designated no-anchoring areas at Gatakers Bay and Gables Point.

Know your zone

Fishing rules and marine park zoning

Start with the official Great Sandy Marine Park maps and check the exact place you intend to fish. Where the map shows a conservation park zone (yellow), current marine park guidance limits line fishing to two lines or rods per person and two hooks or lures per line. Other fisheries size, possession, closure and gear rules also apply.

Designated no-anchoring areas protect reef habitat near Gatakers Bay and Gables Point. Zoning and rules can change, so this guide links to the official sources rather than attempting to reproduce every limit.

Before every trip

  • Find your exact position on the marine-park map, including any designated-area boundary.
  • Open the current Queensland fishing rules for gear, species, size and possession limits, and closures.
  • Identify the catch before keeping it.
  • Retrieve line and tackle; do not leave hooks where wildlife or people can find them.

Quick answers

Common Point Vernon fishing questions

Do I need a recreational fishing licence?

Queensland does not use one general saltwater recreational fishing licence, but permits apply in some stocked impoundments and many rules still govern gear, species and possession. Check the current Queensland rules for your activity.

Can I anchor near Gatakers Bay or Gables Point?

No anchoring is permitted inside the designated Gatakers Bay and Gables Point no-anchoring areas. These are separate from the offshore artificial reef. Use the official map to identify every boundary.

Can I fish near the boat ramp?

Do not obstruct launching, retrieval or the navigation channel. Choose a position where your line cannot cross working boat access.

What is biting now?

Conditions change quickly. Check a recent local fishing report and current rules, then treat it as an indication rather than a guarantee.
