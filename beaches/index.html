Shoreline guide

Point Vernon beaches and foreshore

Point Vernon is not one long swimming beach. Its shoreline changes between narrow sand, broad tidal rock flats, low-energy bays and creek-edge habitat.

Know the coast

Four parts of the Point Vernon shoreline

Surf Life Saving Australia describes Point Vernon as a low rocky promontory surrounded by low-energy beaches. “Low energy” does not mean conditions are always suitable for swimming: these are unpatrolled tidal beaches, and the amount of sand and water in front of you changes markedly through the tide.

Gatakers Bay

An approximately 800-metre beach between Eli Point and central Point Vernon. It has a narrow high-tide strip of sand in front of broad linear rock flats, with a public boat ramp in the bay.

Read the detailed Gatakers Bay guide

Point Vernon beach

An approximately 1.5-kilometre east-facing section running from the northern tip of the point towards Gables Point. Beachsafe records a sandy high-tide beach backed by the foreshore reserve and Esplanade.

Gables Point

A longer south-facing section backed by the Esplanade and foreshore reserve. It has a narrow high-tide beach, broad rock flats and a small public ramp near the eastern tip.

Eli Creek edge

A short north-facing beach near the creek mouth, with tidal flats and scattered mangroves. Creek mouths can have currents and soft ground, so this is a place to observe carefully rather than assume it is a swimming spot.

Water safety

Can you swim at Point Vernon?

People do enter the water around Hervey Bay, but Point Vernon’s beaches are not patrolled by Surf Life Saving Australia. There are no red-and-yellow flags here. Conditions vary with tide, wind, visibility, water depth and the rock or sand underfoot.

Use a cautious rule

If you cannot judge the conditions confidently, do not enter. Never treat a low hazard number on a directory as a guarantee for the day you visit.

  • Check current weather, tide and warnings before leaving.
  • Look for submerged rock, sharp shells and sudden changes in depth.
  • Supervise children closely and keep them within reach around water.
  • Avoid creek mouths, boat channels and ramp approaches.
  • For patrolled swimming, check current flagged-beach information through Beachsafe.

Plan around the water

Tides change the experience

A higher tide can leave only a narrow strip of beach. A lower tide exposes broad rock and sand flats, making the coast feel much larger. The safest route back may not look the same after the tide turns.

Wet rock can be slippery and uneven. Wear secure footwear, avoid taking marine life from pools, and leave shells and animals where they are. Point Vernon sits beside protected Great Sandy Marine Park habitat, including rocky shore and fringing reef communities.

Shared shoreline

Dogs on the foreshore

Council designates signed sections of the Point Vernon foreshore as 24-hour off-leash areas. The boundaries are specific and include exceptions around playground approaches, so use the signs at your access point rather than assuming the whole coast is off leash.

Dogs must remain under effective control. Keep them away from wildlife, people fishing, children, and any signed turtle or shorebird areas.

Quick answers

Common questions about Point Vernon beaches

Is Gatakers Bay a patrolled beach?

No. Beachsafe currently records no Surf Life Saving patrol service at Gatakers Bay.

When are the rock pools visible?

More rock is generally exposed as the tide falls, but the timing and usable access vary. Check a current local tide source and watch conditions on arrival.

Is the foreshore suitable for bare feet?

Some sandy sections may be, but rock platforms can be sharp, slippery or covered by shells. Secure footwear is the safer choice for exploring.

Can I take shells or animals from the rock pools?

Leave living animals and their habitat undisturbed. Marine park and fisheries rules apply to collecting, and some species cannot be taken.
