Visiting Point Vernon

Last updated: 8 September 2026

Start with a wander beside the bay, a picnic at ESA Park or a look at Gatakers Bay’s tidal shoreline. This guide helps you choose a first stop, get here and leave time to enjoy Point Vernon at your own pace.

First time here?

Choose a place to begin

For bay views: Gatakers Bay

Begin near the Corfield Street foreshore for views across the bay and a shoreline that changes with the tide. Enjoy it from the reserve or a suitable section of path; keep clear of the working boat ramp and channel.

Explore Gatakers Bay

For a picnic and play: ESA Park

On the Esplanade between Aplin and Flinders streets, ESA Park brings together a playground, picnic shelters, barbecues and toilets. Council also lists all-abilities toilets. Check the parking and access notes for your needs.

See ESA Park facilities

For local history: The Gables

Combine a foreshore stop with the story of the former boarding house and Dunn’s Enclosure. The historic site is a different place from the much longer beach section called Gables Point.

Discover The Gables story

Point Vernon’s beaches are unpatrolled. Staying on the formed path or in a park is a different outing from entering the water or walking on the tidal rock flats.

Leave room to linger

A relaxed half-day in Point Vernon

For a short visit, choose one stop and enjoy it without rushing. With a half-day available, add a picnic and a history stop from the ideas below. These are suggested outings, with no fixed walking distance or schedule; choose the order to suit your transport and the conditions.

  1. Begin by the water. Take a short out-and-back wander at Gatakers Bay. From the reserve, notice how the tide changes the strip of sand and the exposed rock.
  2. Pause for food or a picnic. Check the local food and coffee guide, or bring a picnic to ESA Park. The playground, shelters and toilets make it a useful family stop.
  3. Add a little history. If you have time, visit The Gables and read about the boarding house that once stood here. Keep clear of vehicles using the nearby Aplin Street boat ramp.

Before linking stops on foot, check the route and allow for the return. If using on-demand transport, confirm the permitted journey and book both ways; it does not offer unrestricted trips between every local stop.

Hervey Bay’s northern edge

Where is Point Vernon?

Point Vernon sits at the north-western end of Hervey Bay’s urban coastline in Queensland’s Fraser Coast region. The Esplanade follows much of the suburb’s foreshore, connecting views of the bay with parks and residential streets.

Choose a named park or street access as your destination. The Point Vernon map and access guide helps you orient the coast; live navigation will give you a route and travel estimate from where you are.

Choose your connection

Driving and public transport

By car

Road access is through Hervey Bay’s urban network. Use live navigation for the final route because construction, school traffic and temporary events can change the most convenient approach. The Esplanade follows much of Point Vernon’s coastline; watch for people crossing between residential streets, parks and the water.

By public transport

Point Vernon does not have a general all-day fixed-route bus. Translink route 765 is marked as a school-only service, so it should not be used to plan an ordinary visitor journey.

Bookable on demand

Translink’s Hervey Bay on-demand service covers all of Point Vernon and must be booked, including the return journey. Translink currently lists operating hours of 7:30am–5:30pm Monday to Saturday, with no Sunday or public-holiday service. Confirm the current fare, booking method and pickup details on the official page before travel. The service connects the roam zone to nominated key locations; it is not a shuttle between every beach or park.

Once you arrive

Parking, walking and cycling

Set a return plan before leaving your first stop: the same car park, a booked pickup point or a comfortable turnaround on foot. Open the named destination links to make meeting up easier.

Parking is dispersed across foreshore and neighbourhood access points rather than concentrated in one visitor car park. Park only where permitted, keep driveways and boat-ramp manoeuvring areas clear, and do not stop on fragile foreshore vegetation.

The shared-path network can connect multiple sections of the foreshore, but surfaces, crossings and continuity vary. Cyclists should slow around families, dogs and blind corners. Use the map and access guide to choose an area, then see the walking and cycling guide for route ideas and the items still needing an on-ground accessibility check.

The coast sets the pace

Tides, weather and facilities

Tide

Low water exposes broad rock and sand flats. Higher water can reduce the usable beach. Boat ramps and walking routes may work differently across the tide.

Wind and sun

The bay can look calm while still being exposed to heat, glare and a fresh onshore breeze. Carry water, sun protection and a light layer.

Facilities

Facilities are concentrated at selected parks. Do not assume toilets, shade or drinking water are available at every shoreline access. Start with the verified accessibility notes if a particular facility is essential.

Swimming

Point Vernon beaches are unpatrolled. Check conditions, supervise children closely and use Beachsafe to find current patrol information elsewhere.

Make a day of it

Where to stay

Point Vernon is predominantly residential, but two holiday parks operate locally. Compare those with the wider Hervey Bay area, then confirm location, accessibility, cancellation terms and current details directly with the provider.

Before you go

A short planning checklist

  • Check the current forecast, local tide-planning advice and any warnings.
  • Confirm your outward and return transport, particularly if visiting on a Sunday, public holiday or after the on-demand service finishes.
  • Bring drinking water, sun protection and footwear suited to the outing; choose a picnic or check food options before you leave.
  • Choose a park with the facilities you need, and check for closures.
  • Keep the visit flexible; tide and wind may make another part of the foreshore more comfortable. If bringing a dog, check the signed foreshore boundaries first.
