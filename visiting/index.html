Visiting Point Vernon

Point Vernon is a low-key coastal suburb rather than a single attraction. A little planning around transport, tides and facilities makes the headland much easier to enjoy.

Hervey Bay’s northern edge

Where is Point Vernon?

Point Vernon occupies the low headland at the north-western end of Hervey Bay’s urban coastline. Queensland Places describes it as about 35 kilometres north of Maryborough. It is part of the Fraser Coast local government area and sits opposite the sheltered waters between the mainland and K’gari, formerly known as Fraser Island.

Road distance and travel time vary with the starting point, route and traffic. Use live navigation for your actual journey rather than relying on a fixed estimate copied into a guide.

Driving and public transport

By car

Road access is through Hervey Bay’s urban network. Use live navigation for the final route because construction, school traffic and temporary events can change the most convenient approach. The Esplanade curves around much of the headland; watch for people crossing between residential streets, parks and the water.

By bus

Translink route 765 serves Point Vernon. Timetables and stop patterns can change, so plan the exact journey through Translink on the day rather than relying on a copied timetable.

On demand

Translink’s Hervey Bay on-demand service currently includes Point Vernon in its roam zone. It requires booking and operates under its own service rules. Check the official page for eligibility, operating times and how to book.

Once you arrive

Parking, walking and cycling

Parking is dispersed across foreshore and neighbourhood access points rather than concentrated in one visitor car park. Park only where permitted, keep driveways and boat-ramp manoeuvring areas clear, and do not stop on fragile foreshore vegetation.

The shared-path network can connect multiple sections of the foreshore, but surfaces, crossings and continuity vary. Cyclists should slow around families, dogs and blind corners. See the walking and cycling guide for a route overview and the items still needing an on-ground accessibility check.

The coast sets the pace

Tides, weather and facilities

Tide

Low water exposes broad rock and sand flats. Higher water can reduce the usable beach. Boat ramps and walking routes may work differently across the tide.

Wind and sun

The bay can look calm while still being exposed to heat, glare and a fresh onshore breeze. Carry water, sun protection and a light layer.

Facilities

Facilities are concentrated at selected parks. Do not assume toilets, shade or drinking water are available at every shoreline access.

Swimming

Point Vernon beaches are unpatrolled. Check conditions, supervise children closely and use Beachsafe to find current patrol information elsewhere.

Accommodation and a simple itinerary

Point Vernon is predominantly residential. For a broader range of visitor accommodation, compare official listings across Hervey Bay and the Fraser Coast. Confirm location, accessibility, cancellation terms and current reviews directly with the provider.

  2. Middle of the day: pause at a park with confirmed shade and facilities.
  3. Later: visit Gatakers Bay or a suitable lookout after checking wind and tide.

Before you go

A short planning checklist

  • Check the current forecast, tide and any local warnings.
  • Confirm public transport or on-demand bookings.
  • Carry water, sun protection and secure shoreline footwear.
  • Choose a park whose listed facilities match your needs.
  • Keep the visit flexible; tide and wind may make another part of the headland more comfortable.
